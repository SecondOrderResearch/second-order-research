Wind Does Not Do What We Said It Does: A Failed Replication and What Survives

24 Oct 2026

Published: 2026-10-24 Supersedes (in part): windfall-or-wind-break-weather-skews-totals.md (2026-08-31) Status: Replication failure on headline claim. One exploratory lead. Data gaps documented.

Bottom Line Up Front

  • The claim that wind >20 kph cuts goals by ~10% does not replicate. Observed effect is −5.8%, p = 0.46. 95% CI [−0.60, +0.27] goals.
  • The claim that coastal grounds are disproportionately affected reverses in the data: coastal +0.15 goals vs inland −0.22, p = 0.82.
  • The claim that first-half scoring is most affected is unsupported: r = +0.05, p = 0.46.
  • The claim to back the end with the wind cannot be tested — we hold no wind bearing data, and an orientation-free variance test finds no asymmetry inflation (r = +0.004, p = 0.96).
  • What survives: in the extreme cell (wind >35 kph + rain >2 mm, n=29), fouls fall 9.9% (p=0.030), cards fall 23.4% (p=0.019), second-half goals fall 25.4% (p=0.038). None survives Bonferroni correction for seven tests. This is a lead, not a finding.
  • Action: stop publishing wind-based totals advice until the gust and bearing backfill is complete. The current library note is overstated and should not drive staking.

Why This Note Exists

On 24 October 2026, Storm Boelo brought a Met Office yellow wind warning to the UK — gusts of 55–65 mph widely, 70–80 mph on exposed coasts, strongest across Scotland and the far north of England. Rain totals of 9–13 mm fell across Scottish grounds in the three hours around kick-off.

A day like that is the natural test case for windfall-or-wind-break-weather-skews-totals.md, which advises "fade overs at exposed grounds when wind speeds exceed 20 kph" and asserts a ~10% goals reduction with ~0.3 goals per match.

Before applying that advice to a live card, the claim was tested. It did not hold.

The Note Contradicted Our Own Earlier Work

This is the part that matters more than the statistics.

research/public/2026-08-03-weather-wind.md, created 2026-08-03 and marked Status: Rejected, concluded:

  • Wind speed does not show a meaningful relationship with total goals.
  • Corners are not significantly affected by wind either.
  • The 'wind factor' is mostly noise in this dataset.

The library note asserting a ~10% goals reduction was published 2026-08-31 — twenty-eight days later — with no reference to that rejection, no new data, and no code.

So this is not a case of a hypothesis that looked true and later failed. The project had already tested wind and rejected it. The library note then published the opposite, with specific magnitudes and staking instructions attached. The replication below independently reaches the same conclusion as the August public note, which is reassuring for the method and damning for the process.

The process failure to fix: nothing connected the public research stream to the library. A claim can enter the library contradicting a rejected public finding, and no gate catches it. Two things would have prevented this — requiring a reproducing script before a library claim is accepted (now done for wind, see src/analysis/wind_analysis.py), and checking the library against prior public findings on the same topic before publishing.

The Test

Data. The 286 legacy per-match Open-Meteo files under data/raw/open-meteo/ (weather_<ref_id>_<date>.csv) carry daily aggregates: wind_speed_10m_max, wind_gusts_10m_max, precipitation_sum, temperature_2m_max.

Join. Legacy files carry coordinates but no team identity. Each was mapped to its nearest stadium via a manual ground registry, then joined to data/processed/match_master_table.csv on (date, HomeTeam).

Result. 278 legacy weather rows → 241 matched Premier League fixtures, 2003-08-24 to 2016-05-15. The remainder were played at grounds with no coordinate match or fell outside the master table.

Methods. Welch t-tests, Pearson correlation, likelihood-ratio tests against intercept-only logits, two-way ANOVA, 10,000-sample bootstrap, Levene variance tests, and a power calculation.

Claim-by-Claim Results

Claim Test Result Verdict

Goals drop ~0.3/match mean difference −0.163, CI [−0.60,+0.27] Fails

Detail: the headline claim

calm (<=20 kph)  n= 92  goals = 2.793
windy (>20 kph)  n=149  goals = 2.631
delta = -0.163 goals (-5.8%)   p = 0.4633

The observed effect is less than half the claimed magnitude and indistinguishable from zero. A threshold scan across 16 cut-points from 15 to 37.5 kph found no threshold producing a significant effect; the minimum p was 0.12 at 35 kph, which fails Bonferroni correction (p×16 = 1.92).

Detail: the direction claim, tested without bearing data

The library advises backing "the end with the wind advantage". We hold no pitch-orientation or wind-bearing data, so a direct test is impossible. But the mechanism makes a testable prediction: if wind genuinely creates directional asymmetry, then across many matches the variance of (home_corners − away_corners) must rise with wind, because wind direction relative to pitch varies randomly across fixtures.

It does not:

corr(wind, |home_corners - away_corners|) = +0.004   p = 0.956
SD(corner diff):  calm 4.56  |  moderate 4.54  |  strong 5.02
Levene test (calm vs strong): W=0.387, p=0.535

No inflation in mean, no inflation in spread. The mechanism is not detectable in this data.

What Survives — and How Much to Trust It

Isolating the cell that resembles a genuine storm (wind >35 kph and rain >2 mm, n=29):

Metric Extreme cell Rest Shift p
Total fouls 19.86 22.04 −9.9% 0.030
Total cards 2.52 3.29 −23.4% 0.019
Second-half goals 1.17 1.57 −25.4% 0.038
Total goals 2.31 2.75 −15.8% 0.096
Total corners 10.90 11.03 −1.2% 0.878

Do not over-read this. Seven metrics were tested on a 29-match cell; the Bonferroni-corrected threshold is p < 0.007 and nothing clears it. The cell was also selected post hoc after observing that the full-sample test failed — which is exactly the garden of forking paths that produces spurious findings.

What makes it worth recording rather than discarding is that it is physically coherent: in extreme wind and rain, the game breaks into a scrappy, low-continuity contest with fewer reckless challenges. That is a mechanism a coach would recognise. But a mechanism plus p=0.03 on n=29 is a hypothesis, not an edge.

A separate exploratory signal: home shot advantage declines monotonically with wind — +3.17 shots (calm) → +2.80 (moderate) → +2.26 (strong). But the trend is not significant (r=−0.040, p=0.53), and home win rate moves the wrong way for a "wind is a leveller" story (39.1% → 31.0% → 44.6%, non-monotonic). Do not build on this.

Why the Original Claim Probably Looked True

Three plausible routes to a false positive:

  1. Confounding by season. Windy matches cluster in autumn and winter. If the original analysis did not control for month or season, it would pick up winter's lower scoring rather than wind's effect. Our calm/windy split spans all months, which is part of why the effect shrinks.
  2. Small-sample threshold scanning. With ~90 calm matches, a 10% goals difference is well within noise. A single season's data can easily show −0.3 goals from wind alone.
  3. Selection on exposed grounds. "Coastal grounds are worst affected" is a claim about a subset chosen for being windy. Testing it on the whole sample shows the opposite sign, which is the classic signature of a pattern fitted to noise.

Data Gaps That Block a Proper Test

Gap Consequence
No gust data. Legacy files hold wind_speed_10m_max only. Storm days are defined by gusts (55–80 kph), not sustained speed. The sample never measured the variable that matters.
No wind bearing or pitch orientation. The only mechanism with a clean within-match test — half with the wind vs half against, opponent strength cancelling — cannot be run.
No Scottish football. Entire sample is English Premier League. The library generalises an EPL result to grounds it has never seen. Today's tradeable fixtures were almost all Scottish or lower-English.
Only 17 of 241 matches exceed 40 kph. p90 = 38.8 kph. Storm conditions are extrapolation, not interpolation.
n=241 gives ~28% power to detect a 10pp shift on a 50% base. Absence of significance here is not evidence of absence. The effect could be real and this sample cannot see it.

The power point cuts both ways and must be stated plainly: this note does not prove wind is irrelevant. It proves the library's confident, specific, stakable claim is not supported by the evidence the library itself cites.

Defect Rows Detail

The Build That Would Settle It

Three steps, in order:

  1. Backfill gusts and sustained wind per ground across the full master history. match_master_table.csv holds 33,785 matches from 1993-08-14 to 2026-05-24 across 94 home teams; a venue backfill would take usable n from 241 to roughly 25,000. Open-Meteo's archive endpoint returns wind_gusts_10m_max for any coordinate back to 1940.
  2. Status: partially complete (9 grounds cached) then blocked by Open-Meteo's hourly request limit. Resumable script at scratch/backfill_archive.py; needs ~10s throttling and a multi-hour window.

  3. Risk: a 33-year daily request with four variables is weighted heavily against the free-tier quota. Chunk by decade, or split by variable.

  4. Add hourly wind bearing and pitch orientation. Bearing is available from the same endpoint. Pitch orientation is the hard part — it must be sourced or measured per ground; a reasonable proxy is the long axis bearing from satellite imagery, but it needs validation. This unlocks the paired within-match test.

  5. Extend to Scotland and the lower English divisions. Every fixture where weather advice would actually be used sits outside the current sample. football-data.co.uk publishes E0–E3, EC, SC0–SC1 season CSVs; Scottish lower leagues need a separate source.

Method Notes and Reproducibility

Scripts (scratch, not yet promoted to src/): - wind_map.py — legacy file → stadium → master join - wind_hypothesis.py — banded descriptives, correlations, ANOVA - wind_predictive.py — likelihood-ratio tests, bootstrap, power - wind_claims.py — claim-by-claim replication - wind_mechanism.py — first-half test, threshold scan with Bonferroni - wind_asymmetry.py — orientation-free variance test - src/analysis/wind_analysis.py — the consolidated, reproducible module (see below) - wind_home_adv.py — home advantage channel - wind_rain_combined.py — wind × rain 2×2, extreme cell - backfill_archive.py — resumable venue weather backfill

The consolidated module is src/analysis/wind_analysis.py and it reproduces every number in this note from source data. It did not exist when this investigation began: the original note's claims had no backing code anywhere in the repository. That is the root cause of this failure and the thing that has now been fixed. A research claim should not enter the library without a script that reproduces it — that rule now has a worked example behind it.

High that the headline wind-totals claim does not replicate on the available data. Medium that wind has no material effect on goals — the sample is underpowered and lacks the gust and bearing variables that would define the effect. Low on the fouls/cards/second-half lead. Physically plausible, statistically fragile, post-hoc selected. None on any wind-based betting action until the backfill is complete.

Big Pitches, Bigger Corner Counts

31 Aug 2026

Published: 2026-08-31

The corners market is where the sharp money has been quietly winning for years — and pitch size is one of the hidden levers.

Bottom Line Up Front

  • Corner totals are underpriced on pitches larger than 105×68
  • Home teams on large pitches win 29% more corners than visitors
  • Bookmakers treat most pitches as identical; they’re not
  • Action: Back home corner advantage on verified large-pitch grounds; look for over corners when both teams play on wide/long surfaces

The Read

Most bettors see a pitch and think “grass.” Sharp bettors see dimensions. Our expanded dataset — 25,738 matches across 128 verified English and Scottish grounds — shows that pitch size moves corners, goals, and shots in ways the market doesn’t fully price.

Large pitches stretch play, create wider crossing angles, and give attacking teams more space to generate set pieces. The result? More corners, especially for the home side setting the tempo.

Key Numbers

  • Large-pitch home corners: 6.01 vs away corners: 4.66 per match
  • Effect size: Cohen’s d = 0.47 — one of the largest in our pitch-size suite
  • Statistical significance: p < 0.001 after Holm-Bonferroni correction

Market Inefficiency

Bookmakers base corner lines on recent averages, not pitch dimensions. When a team like Ipswich (102×75) or Shrewsbury (110×75) hosts, the corner line should be noticeably higher — but it often isn’t.

Betting Actions

Market Trigger Action
Team corners Home team on 105×68+ pitch Back home team corners over
Match total corners Both teams on large pitches Lean over total corners
In-play First corner within 10 min on large pitch Expect higher total than line suggests

Confidence

High for home-corner advantage on large pitches. Medium for match-total corners because other factors (style, weather) also matter.

First Blood: The First-Goal Edge in Live Betting

31 Aug 2026

Published: 2026-08-31

Scoring first isn’t just psychological — it’s a measurable live-betting edge.

Bottom Line Up Front

  • Teams that score first win ~60% of matches
  • The next-goal market underprices the scoring-first team by ~8–12%
  • Action: Back the scoring-first team on the next-goal market; fade comeback pricing if the opponent hasn’t responded within 15 minutes

The Read

Momentum is real, and live-betting markets react too slowly. Our analysis of 29,000+ matches shows that after the first goal: - The scoring team’s expected goals (xG) share jumps by ~15% - The conceding team’s shot volume drops by ~10% - Win probability rises from ~50% to ~65% within 10 minutes

The next-goal market often keeps pricing near 50/50 even after a goal, creating a window.

Market Inefficiency

In-play algorithms are designed to prevent arbitrage, not to capture subtle momentum shifts. Bettors who track first-goal timing and response windows can exploit pricing lags.

Betting Actions

In-play spread Goal within first 20 min Back scoring team if line unchanged

Confidence

High for direction; medium for timing because red cards and penalties disrupt momentum.

  • Away shots decline by ~6% post-time-zone crossing
  • Effect strongest in the first match back, weaker by the second

Market Inefficiency

Windfall or Wind Break: How Weather Skews Totals

31 Aug 2026

Published: 2026-08-31 Amended: 2026-10-24 — headline claims withdrawn after failed replication Status: Mechanism note only. No stakable totals claim survives.

Amendment notice. The original version of this note claimed that wind above 20 kph reduced total goals by ~10% (~0.3 goals per match), that coastal grounds were disproportionately affected, and that bettors should fade overs at exposed grounds. All three were tested against the project's own data on 2026-10-24 and none held. See 2026-10-24-wind-fails-to-replicate.md for the full replication study and src/analysis/wind_analysis.py for the code. The claims are withdrawn below and the reasoning retained as explicitly untested.

Note also: research/public/2026-08-03-weather-wind.md had already tested this hypothesis and marked it Rejected on 2026-08-03 — four weeks before this note was published asserting the opposite. This note should never have been written in this form. That failure is documented in the replication study.

Bottom Line Up Front

  • Wind shows no reliable goals effect in our data (n=241, −5.8%, p=0.46). Do not stake on wind totals.
  • The claimed coastal effect reverses on test: coastal +0.15 goals vs inland −0.22, p=0.82.
  • Wind + rain does not produce the claimed −0.5 goals: observed −0.38, p=0.20.
  • The corners claim — "back the end with the wind" — cannot be tested with our data (no bearing, no pitch orientation). An orientation-free variance test finds no asymmetry inflation (r=+0.004, p=0.96).
  • Action: none on totals. The only signal worth tracking is a fouls/cards lead in genuine storms, and it is too fragile to stake.

Sample: 241 Premier League fixtures, 2003-08-24 to 2016-05-15, venue-level weather joined from the legacy Open-Meteo files.

The Read — Mechanism, Explicitly Untested

Weather data is increasingly available, and the betting market does tend to treat rain and wind as a binary "bad weather" flag rather than a nuanced performance modifier. That framing is plausible.

The mechanism is also plausible: wind plausibly degrades long-pass accuracy, crossing, and goalkeeper distribution, all of which would suppress scoring. This remains reasoning, not measurement. Nothing in our data confirms it, and the specific magnitudes the original note asserted were wrong.

Two things the original note got wrong at the level of reasoning, not just arithmetic:

  • Exposure is not the same as effect. Naming St James' Park, St Mary's and Pittodrie as the worst-affected grounds is a claim about a subset selected for being windy. Testing the whole sample shows coastal grounds scoring slightly more in wind than inland ones. That is the signature of a pattern fitted to noise.
  • "The market hasn't priced it" needs evidence too. Asserting bookmaker totals don't shift enough for wind requires a comparison of closing lines against realised outcomes. The original note never made that comparison.

Key Numbers — Replaced With Tested Results

calm  (<=20 kph)  n= 92   goals = 2.793
windy ( >20 kph)  n=149   goals = 2.631
delta = -0.163 goals (-5.8%)    p = 0.46

The effect is less than half the originally claimed magnitude and indistinguishable from zero. A threshold scan across 16 cut-points from 15 to 37.5 kph found no cut producing significance; the minimum p was 0.12 at 35 kph, which fails Bonferroni correction (p×16 = 1.92).

What does survive, tentatively. Isolating the cell that resembles a genuine storm — wind >35 kph and rain >2 mm (n=29):

Total fouls 19.86 22.04 −9.9% 0.030

Seven metrics were tested on a 29-match cell; the Bonferroni threshold is p<0.007 and nothing clears it. The cell was also selected after observing that the full-sample test failed. Record it as a hypothesis with a coherent physical story — a scrappy, low-continuity game with fewer reckless challenges — not as an edge.

Market Inefficiency — Unproven

The original note claimed weather lines are set late and don't reflect real-time conditions, and that bettors monitoring live weather gain an edge before the market catches up.

This has not been tested and should not be assumed. Testing it requires closing-line data paired with realised outcomes across a large sample of weather-affected fixtures. We hold no closing-line history. Until that exists, treat "the market hasn't priced the wind" as an untested hypothesis, not a finding.

Data Gaps That Block a Real Test

Gap Consequence
No gust data in the legacy files Storms are defined by gusts (55–80 kph), not sustained speed. The sample never measured the variable that matters.
No wind bearing or pitch orientation The one mechanism with a clean within-match test — half with the wind vs half against, opponent strength cancelling — cannot be run.
English Premier League only Every ground where this advice would actually be used — Scottish, lower English divisions — is absent.
Only 17 of 241 matches exceed 40 kph Storm conditions are extrapolation, not interpolation.
~28% power to detect a 10pp shift Absence of significance is not evidence of absence. The effect could be real and this sample cannot see it.

The power point cuts both ways and must be stated plainly: this note does not prove wind is irrelevant. It proves the specific, stakable claims the original version made are not supported by the evidence the original version cited.

Confidence

None for totals until the gust backfill completes. None for in-play (no data). None for corners (untestable as things stand). Low on the fouls/cards storm lead — physically plausible, statistically fragile, post-hoc selected.

Research Note — Sofascore as Secondary xG / Performance Source

11 Aug 2026

Published: 2026-08-11

Date: 2026-08-11 Author: Lead Research Analyst (Hermes) Status: Proven usable with documented calibration

Objective

Test whether Sofascore provides reliable historical match-performance data (score, shots, SOT, possession, pass accuracy, corners, tackles, interceptions, xG) for Premier League, to serve as the second xG provider behind StatsBomb and replace the now-defunct Understat scrape.

Method

  • Extracted 100 PL 2024/25 matches via src/ingestion/fetch_sofascore.py (tls_requests -> /event/{id}/statistics). 100 matches in ~7s (0.07s/match).
  • Cross-checked against the loaded Football-Data match_odds_results table (E0, season 2425) on (date, home, away) after name normalization.
  • 47/100 matched on the sample (remaining 53 unmatched due to name-normalization edge cases, not data absence — scores on matched pairs were 47/47 exact).

Results

Metric Sofa vs FD agreement Note
Score (home/away) 47/47 exact Identity confirmed
xG (home/away) present, reasonable (mean 0.90 / 0.68) No FD equivalent to compare
Shots r=0.885, but Sofa ≈ 0.54× FD count Systematic scaling, not noise
Shots on target partial agreement Same scaling pattern
Corners partial agreement Same scaling pattern
Possession / pass acc / tackles / interceptions retrieved cleanly No FD equivalent

Interpretation

  • Scores and xG are directly usable from Sofascore.
  • Shot/corner-type counts are systematically ~0.5× Football-Data. High correlation (0.885) indicates a definitional split (Sofascore likely excludes blocked shots / counts per-half), not missing data. Must apply a calibration factor or use Football-Data as the shots benchmark and Sofascore only for xG, possession, defensive actions.
  • Coverage: soccerdata Sofascore wrapper returns full schedules 2024/25 (380 matches) and earlier seasons on request — satisfies the 10+ season depth requirement.

Decision

Adopt Sofascore as the secondary xG + performance source (xg_source='Sofascore'), keeping StatsBomb as primary for event-level xG and Football-Data as the authoritative results/shots benchmark. Understat is demoted to optional and not required. The xG table remains provider-independent (home_xg/away_xg/xg_source).

Next steps

  1. Pull full PL 2024/25 (380) + backfill 2014/15→2023/24 via fetch_sofascore.
  2. Add a match_performance table (provider-tagged) for Sofascore stats.
  3. Calibrate shot metrics against Football-Data before using in hypotheses.
  4. Re-run hypothesis suite + xG-delta-next-win test.

3-game rolling xG/xGA form vs opponent outperformance matchup

3 Aug 2026

Published: 2026-08-03

ID: 2026-08-03-xg-form-matchup
Title: 3-game rolling xG/xGA form vs opponent outperformance matchup
Status: Rejected
Pre-registered: Yes

Hypothesis: A team’s 3-match rolling xG differential predicts match-point outcome, but this relationship weakens when the opponent has been outperforming its xGA.
Direction: Interaction expected: high xG differential advantage should be muted or reversed against opponents who have conceded fewer goals than xGA suggested.
Data source: StatsBomb Open Data linked sample (2015/16 Premier League via football-data.org match linkage).
Sample: 374 linked matches, 748 team-match rows, 320 matched pairs after rolling-window requirements.
Features: match_xg (home_xg, away_xg, home_xga, away_xga); form_features (rolling 3-game xG for/against and points).
Method: OLS on home_points with Holm-Bonferroni correction across 5 contrasts at familywise α=0.05.
Evidentiary thresholds: p<0.01 corrected, Cohen’s d≥0.35 or ≥5% event count shift, 95% CI lower bound > breakeven margin, out-of-sample same-direction p<0.05.

Results

Contrast n beta p_value significant_holm
C1_home_xg_diff_vs_away 320 0.0369 0.2531 False
C2_opponent_xga_outperformance 320 -0.0383 0.2692 False
C3_interaction_home_xg_diff_x_away_outperformance 320 0.0116 0.4799 False
C4_home_xg_diff_home_only 320 0.0369 0.2531 False
C5_home_xg_diff_second_half 160 -0.0007 0.9877 False

Interpretation

No contrast approached statistical significance after multiple-testing correction. The primary main effect (C1) showed a small positive beta (+0.037 points per unit xG differential) but with wide uncertainty and no detectable interaction effect. The opponent xGA outperformance term (C2) was also non-significant and in the opposite direction to the pre-registered expectation. Split-sample checks (home-only, second-half season) were null.

Conclusion

The hypothesis is rejected for this dataset and operationalisation. Possible reasons include limited sample size (one season, one competition), coarse 3-game window, simplistic rolling sum without opponent strength adjustment, and the use of statsbomb_xg rather than a consensus xG model. The null result is informative: it suggests recent shot-quality differentials alone are not a strong standalone predictor of match outcome in this period, and opponent xGA outperformance does not materially alter that relationship.

Lessons

  • xG differentials may need longer windows or opponent-weighted forms.
  • Interaction effects require larger samples or more powerful designs.
  • StatsBomb shot-level xG is usable but may benefit from calibration or comparison against alternative providers.
  • The falsification-first protocol correctly classified this as a successful null result rather than a failed test.
