# Miles Matter: How Travel Breaks Away Teams

**Bookmakers adjust for jet lag. They don’t adjust for bus-ride grind.**

## Bottom Line Up Front
- Long-distance travel suppresses away performance more than spreads reflect
- Cross-time-zone trips are especially damaging in the first 3 days after arrival
- **Action:** Fade away favorites when they’ve traveled >2,000 miles or crossed time zones within 72 hours

## The Read
Home advantage is partly a travel effect. Our research across 29,000+ matches shows that when away teams cross time zones or cover serious distance, their shot output drops, corners decline, and win probability underperforms the spread.

Bookmakers factor in “travel fatigue” broadly, but they underweight:
- **East-west distance** vs north-south
- **Time-zone transitions** >2 hours
- **Short turnaround** after long trips

## Key Numbers
- Away goals drop by ~8% after trips >2,000 miles
- Away shots decline by ~6% post-time-zone crossing
- Effect strongest in the first match back, weaker by the second

## Market Inefficiency
The spread is set by market consensus, not granular travel data. Sharp bettors who track travel distance, time zones, and recovery time can stay ahead of line movement.

## Betting Actions
| Market | Trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Spread | Away favorite after >2,000-mile trip | Lean home side or fade spread |
| Total goals | Post-long-trip away game | Lean under when both teams traveled |
| In-play | Away team sluggish start post-travel | Live bet home side if line hasn’t moved |

## Confidence
**Medium** for spread impact; **low** for totals because weather and style overlap.
